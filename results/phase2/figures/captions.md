# Phase 2 figure captions

Keyed by figure file name (PNG and PDF share the name). Each caption is shown under its figure in `notebooks/phase2_composition_nets.ipynb` and reused by the report.

## 2.1_val_pred_vs_true

**Both composition networks track the measured cell-type fractions on validation about as closely as the ridge probe, with $C_S$ far more accurate than $C_B$.** Each panel plots predicted against measured fraction for one of the six cell-type classes on the 135 validation pairs from 14 donors: (a) $C_B$, the bulk composition network, on whole-blood bulk profiles (orange); (b) $C_S$, the single-cell composition network, on single-cell pseudobulk profiles (blue). Gray points are the ridge probe, the linear readout of the evaluation protocol, fit on all training pairs of the same domain. The measured fraction is the PBMC-relative share of each class among the cells of the paired single-cell sample; "Other" pools platelets, erythrocytes, progenitors, and innate lymphoid cells. The dashed line is y = x, and both axes of a panel share the same range. The text in each panel gives the per-class R² (1 − residual sum of squares / total sum of squares across samples) of the network and of the ridge probe. Averaged over the six classes, composition R² is 0.474 for $C_B$ and 0.837 for $C_S$, against 0.481 and 0.858 for the ridge probe; the weakest classes are the two rarest in bulk, dendritic cells (R² 0.309) and Other (0.161).

## 2.3_attack

**Small adversarial perturbations fool the composition networks more easily than the ridge probe; the marker probe is about as easy to fool as the networks.** For each validation profile (135 per domain, from 14 donors) a projected-gradient attack searches for a perturbation δ, with L2 norm at most ε times the median training deviation of the domain, that moves the readout's prediction toward the measured composition of a sample from a different donor. The y-axis is the median gap closure, 1 − ‖C(x + δ) − c_t‖₁ / ‖C(x) − c_t‖₁, where C is the readout and c_t the target composition; at each budget the best perturbation over all budgets up to ε is used. (a) Whole-blood bulk inputs: $C_B$, the ridge probe, and the marker probe (ridge on 28 lineage marker genes). (b) Single-cell pseudobulk inputs: $C_S$ and the same two probes refit on that domain. The dashed horizontal line marks half the gap closed; the dotted vertical line at ε = 0.03 is the minimum median ε₅₀ a network had to reach during selection. ε₅₀, given in the legend, is the median over samples of the smallest budget that closes half the gap. The networks need about 60–80% of the ridge probe's budget (ε₅₀ 0.031 vs. 0.050 for bulk, 0.046 vs. 0.060 for single-cell), and both stay above the 0.03 bound; the marker probe reaches 0.033 and 0.026.

## 2.4_robustness_val

**$C_B$ reads composition from translated bulk profiles about as well as the ridge and marker probes, but $C_S$ collapses on single-cell profiles translated from bulk by mean shift.** Composition R² (mean over the six classes of the per-class R²; higher is better) on the 135 validation pairs from 14 donors, when each readout is applied to translations of the validation sources into its domain: (a) $C_B$ on single-cell → bulk translations; (b) $C_S$ on bulk → single-cell translations. Mean shift and CORAL are unpaired baselines fit on donor-disjoint halves of the training donors (single-cell profiles from half A, bulk profiles from half B), the setting of the models trained without pairs. Ridge + spread is fit on all training pairs and is shown, right of the dotted line, as a paired reference. Bars compare the network (orange or blue), the ridge probe (gray), and the marker probe (green) on the same translations. The y-axis is cut at −0.5: hatched bars fall below the cut and their true values are printed on them. On mean-shift translations, $C_S$ reaches −73.5, against −5.0 for the ridge probe and −1.5 for the marker probe; the loss comes almost entirely from the rare "Other" class (per-class R² −437.8), not from a large error in the fractions.
